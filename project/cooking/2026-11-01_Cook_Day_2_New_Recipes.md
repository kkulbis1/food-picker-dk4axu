## 1. Braised Bone-In Short Ribs — wine-free (3 two-person meals)

### Sources and techniques kept
- **America's Test Kitchen / Cook's Country** braised short rib recipes. Their own pages describe **bone-in English-style** short ribs as the preferred cut, a long low braise (their stovetop version runs about 3½–4 h), and 5 lb of bone-in ribs as a standard batch ([ATK how-to](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/how_tos/5411-short-ribs), [ATK braised short ribs](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/4591-braised-beef-short-ribs), [ATK slow-cooker version](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/10688-slow-cooker-red-wine-braised-short-ribs)). *The full ATK pages are members-only. Jay read only the public summaries, not their ingredient amounts.*
- **Serious Eats** technique: bloom unflavored **gelatin** in store-bought stock so the braising liquid gets the body that bones and homemade stock would give it (seen via secondary copies of their short-rib recipe; Jay didn't open the original page).
- Classic braise steps kept: dry and season the meat → **hard sear** for fond and browning → soften the aromatics → **brown the tomato paste** → **reduce** the deglazing liquid → braise covered at a low oven temperature → **chill and lift off the fat** → reduce the sauce.

### Adaptations and why each should work
| Change | Replaces | Why it should work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 cup unsalted beef broth **reduced by half** in the pan, plus **gelatin** | 1–2 cups red wine | Wine gives a braise body, depth and acidity. A reduced, gelatin-enriched stock restores the body and savoriness. Acidity comes separately (next row) |
| **Red wine vinegar in two stages:** 1 Tbsp in the braise, ½–1 tsp tasted in at the end | Wine's acidity | Some acid cooked in mellows into the background. A small amount at the end gives brightness you can control. The fermented alcohol is converted to acetic acid in vinegar; it isn't wine. (Use apple cider vinegar if you'd rather avoid the name.) |
| **2 Tbsp coconut aminos** | Worcestershire/soy that some versions use | Savory and slightly sweet. No alcohol, gluten or fish. Already in the cart |
| **No added sugar**, carrots only | Sugar/honey in some versions | Coconut aminos and carrots add enough sweetness. This keeps the gravy from going sweet |
| ¾ tsp kosher salt total, unsalted broth | Salted broth + generous salt | Less sodium. Most of the flavor comes from the sear, fond, tomato paste and reduction. **Taste the finished sauce and add salt a pinch at a time if it's flat**, so it doesn't end up bland |
| Fat skimmed after chilling | — | Short ribs render a lot of fat. Lifting it off when solid makes a cleaner, lighter sauce |

### Ingredients
- **5 lb bone-in English-cut beef short ribs** (6–8 pieces, about 3 inches long). Trim thick fat caps.
- ¾ tsp kosher salt, ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 Tbsp olive oil
- 2 medium yellow onions, 2 carrots, 2 celery ribs, roughly chopped (pulse in the food processor)
- 2 Tbsp tomato paste (a flavor base, not a tomato sauce)
- 2 cloves garlic, smashed (mild)
- 3 cups **unsalted beef broth**, divided
- 1 packet (2¼ tsp) **unflavored gelatin** (e.g. Knox)
- 2 Tbsp coconut aminos
- 1 Tbsp + 1 tsp red wine vinegar (or apple cider vinegar)
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme or 1 tsp dried; 2 bay leaves
- Optional: 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 Tbsp water, only if the reduced sauce is still thin

**Equipment:** 6+ qt Dutch oven with a lid, oven, tongs, fat separator or a wide container, instant-read thermometer.

### Method
1. **Bloom:** sprinkle the gelatin over ½ cup cold broth in a bowl and leave it.
2. **Season and sear:** heat the oven to **300°F** with a rack in the lower middle. Pat the ribs very dry and season them. Heat the oil in the Dutch oven over medium-high. Sear the ribs in **2–3 batches without crowding**, 3–4 min per side, until deep brown (about 20 min total). Move them to a sheet pan. Pour off all but 1 Tbsp of fat. *(This is raw meat. Use clean tongs afterward.)*
3. **Aromatics:** cook the onion, carrot and celery over medium for 6–8 min until softened and browned at the edges. Add the tomato paste and garlic and stir 2 min until the paste darkens to brick red.
4. **Wine replacement:** pour in 1 cup broth. Scrape up all the brown bits and simmer until reduced by about half (4–5 min).
5. **Braise:** add the remaining 1½ cups broth, the gelatin mixture, coconut aminos, 1 Tbsp vinegar, thyme and bay. Return the ribs, bone side up, in a single layer if possible. The liquid should come about ⅔ up the meat; add water if needed. Bring to a simmer, cover and move to the oven.
6. **Oven 300°F, 3 to 3½ h**, until a fork slides into the meat with almost no resistance. *Safety: the meat is far past the USDA 145°F minimum by this point. Tenderness, not temperature, is the doneness test here.*
7. **Cool fast:** lift the ribs into a shallow container. Strain the liquid into a separate container and press the vegetables to get the juice out; discard the solids. Cool both uncovered for up to 30 min, then **refrigerate within 2 hours** of coming out of the oven. Chill overnight.
8. **Monday (~15 min):** lift off the solid fat. Simmer the sauce 10–15 min until it lightly coats a spoon (about 2 cups). Taste, then add ½–1 tsp vinegar, pepper and salt only if needed. Thicken with the cornstarch slurry only if it's still thin.
9. **Pack:** about 2 ribs + ⅔ cup sauce per container (3 two-person meals). Label them: *SHORT RIBS — fully cooked. Thaw overnight in the fridge. Reheat covered at 325°F for 30–40 min (or simmer gently) to **165°F**. Best quality within 3 months.*

**Freezing:** the meat and sauce freeze together well. Gelatin-rich braises reheat smoothly. **Serve with** fresh mashed potatoes (a household Love) or GF noodles, plus a steam-in-bag vegetable. Don't freeze mashed potatoes in the same container; their texture suffers.
**Estimated sodium:** about 650–750 mg per serving (salt, coconut aminos, unsalted broth, natural beef sodium). Check the labels.
**Pressure-cooker option (Midea), not recommended for the first batch:** high pressure about 45 min + 15 min natural release, then reduce the sauce on sauté. It's faster, but the sauce is thinner and the meat can turn stringy. The oven braise is more forgiving.

## 2. Creamy Ground Beef Noodles — stroganoff-style (3 two-person meals)

### Sources and techniques kept
- **America's Test Kitchen "Ground Beef Stroganoff"** (2017; 85% lean beef, mushrooms, sour cream, egg noodles; about 55 min; serves 4) ([ATK](https://www.americastestkitchen.com/recipes/10274-ground-beef-stroganoff)). *Members-only. Jay saw only the summary, not the amounts or method.*
- Classic stroganoff technique: **brown** the beef well; **brown the mushrooms separately** until their liquid has cooked off (this is where most of the flavor comes from); a flour-thickened broth sauce with paprika, mustard and a savory "Worcestershire" note; **sour cream stirred in off the heat** so it doesn't curdle.

### Adaptations and why each should work
| Change | Replaces | Why it should work |
|---|---|---|
| **2 Tbsp coconut aminos + 1 tsp red wine vinegar** | Worcestershire (Lea & Perrins contains **anchovies**) | Worcestershire supplies savory-sweet depth and tang. Coconut aminos covers the savory-sweet; vinegar covers the tang. No fish, gluten or alcohol |
| **Spicy brown mustard** (check the label: no wine) | Dijon | Many Dijons list **white wine**. Brown mustard gives the same sharp lift at 2 tsp; it's mild in this quantity |
| **GF 1-to-1 flour** (3 Tbsp) | Wheat flour | Thickens a broth sauce the same way. Already in the cart |
| **GF wide noodles or rotini, cooked fresh at serving** | Egg noodles frozen in the sauce | GF pasta goes mushy when frozen in sauce. Fresh noodles take 10 min |
| **90/10 lean beef**, browned in 2 batches | 85/15 | Less saturated fat; batch-browning makes up the flavor. Drain the fat after browning |
| **Olive oil** for browning | Butter | Butter substitutes are ~35% water, so they steam rather than brown. If Smart Balance is at home, a 1 Tbsp swirl at the end is optional |
| Sour cream added **after** reheating | Stirred in before freezing | Dairy-thickened sauces separate when frozen and reheated. Adding it fresh keeps the sauce creamy |

### Ingredients
- **2 lb 90/10 ground beef**
- 1½ lb sliced cremini mushrooms
- 1 large yellow onion, diced
- 2 Tbsp olive oil, divided
- 2 cloves garlic, minced (or ½ tsp garlic powder)
- 1½ tsp sweet paprika (not smoked or hot), ½ tsp dried thyme
- 3 Tbsp GF 1-to-1 flour
- 2½ cups unsalted beef broth
- 2 Tbsp coconut aminos, 1 tsp red wine vinegar, 2 tsp spicy brown mustard (no wine on the label)
- ½ tsp kosher salt, ½ tsp pepper
- **At serving:** ⅓ cup sour cream per two-person meal (1 cup total), 4 oz dry GF noodles per two-person meal (12 oz total), chopped parsley optional

**Equipment:** Dutch oven or 12-inch skillet, colander, thermometer.

### Method
1. **Beef:** heat 1 Tbsp oil over medium-high. Brown the beef in **2 batches**, breaking it into bite-size pieces and letting it sit to develop a crust. It's done at **160°F** with no pink (USDA, ground beef). Drain the fat and set the beef aside.
2. **Mushrooms:** add 1 Tbsp oil, the mushrooms and the onion. Cook **12–15 min**, stirring now and then, until the liquid evaporates and the mushrooms brown.
3. **Flavor:** add the garlic, paprika and thyme for 1 min. Sprinkle in the flour and stir 1 min.
4. **Sauce:** whisk in the broth, coconut aminos, vinegar and mustard. Simmer 3–4 min until thickened. Return the beef with its juices, add the salt and pepper, and simmer 5 min. **Taste.** If it's flat, add salt a pinch at a time and ½ tsp more vinegar.
5. **Store (no sour cream, no noodles):** divide into 3 containers of about 2½ cups each. Fridge 1 (eat within 3–4 days) and freeze 2. Cool and refrigerate within 2 hours.
6. **Serving (about 12 min):** cook 4 oz GF noodles. Reheat a portion of sauce to **165°F** (steaming). **Take it off the heat**, then stir in ⅓ cup sour cream. Spoon over the noodles.

**Label:** *BEEF STROGANOFF SAUCE — fully cooked. Thaw in the fridge. Reheat to 165°F, then off heat stir in ⅓ cup sour cream. Serve over fresh GF noodles. Fridge 3–4 days / freezer 3 months.*
**Estimated sodium:** about 550–600 mg per serving with the sour cream. Check the labels.
